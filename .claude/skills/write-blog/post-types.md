# Post types

Four shapes cover most of what Nayana writes. They're starting points, not forms. If her
material wants a different shape, use the different shape and add it to the bottom of this
file.

Pick one, say which you picked, and move on.

---

## 1. Explainer

**"What this standard actually requires."** CSRD, ESRS, double materiality, GRI vs BRSR,
Scope 3 categories, EU Taxonomy, the Omnibus changes.

**1,200 to 2,000 words.** Longer than this and nobody finishes it.

Her angle on explainers is the audit angle: not "here is what the standard says" but "here
is what it actually requires you to produce, and here's where people fudge it." Anyone can
summarise a standard. She's read the disclosures.

```
Open with the thing that's confusing, or a number that shouldn't be true
The one-sentence version (say it plainly before you complicate it)
What it actually requires
  - the concrete obligation
  - what counts as evidence
  - a worked example or a table
Where people get it wrong  ← the section only she can write
Who this applies to, and from when   ← research this, don't guess
What I'd do first
Close on a thought
```

**Must have:** a table or a worked example. **Must not have:** a dictionary definition
opening, or a neutral both-sides summary with no opinion in it.

Requires the `esg-researcher` agent. Always.

## 2. Career narrative

**Her own story.** Chartered Accountant to ESG. The Edinburgh MSc. Job-hunting in Austria
as a non-EU professional. Learning German. Interviews. Volunteering since she was 10.

**1,000 to 1,600 words.** Story-led, not advice-led.

The honest version is always better than the LinkedIn version. If something was hard, say
it was hard. If she got rejected, say she got rejected. The reason anyone reads these is
that most career writing is polished into uselessness.

```
Straight into a moment. A specific one, with a date or a place.
What I thought was going to happen
What actually happened
The bit I got wrong
What I'd tell someone at the same point   ← concrete, not inspirational
Close on something unresolved, or a fragment
```

**Must have:** at least one thing that didn't work out. **Must not have:** a triumphant
arc, "everything happens for a reason", or advice she hasn't personally tested.

Never invent a moment. Every anecdote comes from her dump or gets left out.

---

## 3. How-to

**Something she has actually built or run.** A carbon footprint model in Excel. Running a
materiality assessment. Reading a sustainability report properly. Setting up a Scope 3
screening.

**1,500 to 2,500 words.** Detailed enough to follow, and it has to actually work.

```
What you'll have at the end, and roughly how long it takes
What you need before you start
The steps, numbered, each one doing one thing
  - with the actual formula, threshold, or source
  - and a table where a table helps
A worked example with real numbers all the way through
Where this breaks, and what to do about it
What I'd add if I had more time
```

**Must have:** a worked example with numbers that add up. If she gave a spreadsheet, the
formulas go in. **Must not have:** steps she's never actually done.

Excel formulas and calculations go in fenced blocks marked `text`, or in tables. Not
`bash`, not `python`.

---

## 4. Opinion

**A position, argued.** Do ESG ratings mean anything. Greenwashing. Whether the Omnibus
rollback matters. Why sustainability data wouldn't survive a financial audit.

**700 to 1,200 words.** Short and sharp. These are the ones that get read.

The whole point is having a view. A balanced survey with no conclusion is a waste of
everyone's time, and it isn't how she talks about this stuff anyway.

```
The claim, in the first three sentences. No throat-clearing.
Why people believe the opposite   ← take it seriously, it's the strongest version of the piece
Why I think they're wrong, with evidence
The bit where I might be wrong    ← honest, not a rhetorical hedge
So what
```

**Must have:** a position stated clearly, and a genuine concession. **Must not have:**
outrage, "we need to do better", or a conclusion that agrees with everybody.

If the claim rests on a regulatory fact, check it with `esg-researcher` first. An opinion
built on a wrong date is embarrassing.

## Other shapes

Not everything fits four boxes. Some that would suit her:

- **Book or report note.** Short, 500 to 800 words, reacting to something she's read.
- **Annotated example.** Take one real sustainability report and mark it up. Very her.
- **The list she keeps in her head.** Ten things she checks in every ESG disclosure.
- **Answering the same question again.** Something she gets asked repeatedly, written down
  properly once.

Add new shapes here as she uses them, with the same level of detail.

---

## Length, honestly

If the material only supports 800 words, write 800 words. Padding a post to hit a target is
the fastest way to make it sound generated. Nayana would rather publish something short
that's true than something long that's filler.
